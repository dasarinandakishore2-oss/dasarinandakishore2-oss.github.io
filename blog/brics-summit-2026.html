BRICS Summit 2026: Theme, Members, Key Highlights & What It Means for India

10 min read · Current Affairs · Back to all guides

Advertisement

The 18th BRICS Summit took place on September 11–12, 2026 at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, with India hosting as the Chair of BRICS 2026. This is one of the most important diplomatic events of the year — and also a common topic in competitive exams, GK tests, and college assignments. Here's everything you need to know, explained clearly.

What is BRICS?

BRICS is a group of major emerging economies that work together on trade, finance, development, and global governance. The name originally stood for the first five members:

  • B — Brazil
  • R — Russia
  • I — India
  • C — China
  • S — South Africa

The group was founded in 2006 as a forum for these large, fast-growing economies to coordinate on global issues — trade rules, financial systems, climate, and security — outside Western-dominated institutions like the G7.

BRICS 2026 Theme

"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability"

India chose this theme for its 2026 chairship, inspired by PM Modi's "Humanity First, People-Centric" vision. The theme focuses on four ideas: making economies more resilient to global shocks, driving innovation, deepening cooperation between member countries, and building sustainably for future generations.

Key Highlights of BRICS Summit 2026

1. New Delhi Declaration 2026

All 11 member countries unanimously adopted the New Delhi Declaration 2026 — a joint statement covering the bloc's positions on major global issues. Key points in the declaration:

  • Deep concern over escalating tensions in West Asia (Middle East)
  • Called for "maximum restraint" to protect global trade, supply chains, and energy security
  • Firm opposition to forced displacement of Palestinians
  • Support for reform of global financial institutions
  • Commitment to cooperation on AI governance

2. Modi–Xi Bilateral Meeting

One of the most watched moments of the summit was the bilateral meeting between PM Narendra Modi and Chinese President Xi Jinping on September 12, 2026 — Xi's first visit to India in seven years. The meeting focused on resetting India-China relations that were severely strained after the deadly Galwan Valley border clash in 2020.

Why this matters: Xi stated that China approaches its relationship with India from a "strategic and long-term perspective." Both leaders stressed their roles as major Global South countries and called for high-quality cooperation toward a multipolar world. Analysts see this as a significant step toward stabilizing the volatile India-China relationship.

3. Russia and Global South

The summit gave Russian President Vladimir Putin an opportunity to strengthen ties with Global South countries, demonstrating that Russia is not isolated despite Western sanctions. Analysts noted that both Russia and China used the platform to appeal to countries looking to hedge against uncertain US trade and security policies.

4. Trade and De-dollarisation

BRICS members continued discussions on reducing dependence on the US dollar in trade between member countries — a long-running topic in the bloc. While no single BRICS currency was announced, members discussed expanding use of local currencies in bilateral trade.

5. Timing Before Xi-Trump Summit

The BRICS Summit came just before the scheduled second China-US summit of 2026, where Xi Jinping is expected to meet Donald Trump on September 24. This made the New Delhi summit strategically significant — it allowed China to show its strength within the Global South bloc before entering high-stakes talks with the US.

Why does BRICS matter for India?

India is one of the five founding members of BRICS and chaired the summit in 2026 for the fourth time. Here's why it matters:

  • Global voice: BRICS gives India a platform to shape global rules alongside other major powers — outside Western-dominated groups like G7 where India is not a member.
  • Trade: BRICS countries together account for a significant portion of global GDP and trade. Strengthening trade ties within BRICS directly benefits Indian exports.
  • New Development Bank (NDB): BRICS established its own development bank (headquartered in Shanghai) that funds infrastructure projects in member countries — including India.
  • Diplomatic positioning: Hosting the summit gave India the opportunity to reset relations with China, manage ties with Russia despite Western pressure, and strengthen links with Middle Eastern members like Saudi Arabia and UAE.
BRICS countries together represent about 45% of the world's population and over 35% of global GDP — making it one of the most powerful blocs in the world.

Remember these for GK/competitive exams:

• 2026 Summit: 18th Summit, New Delhi, Bharat Mandapam
• Host: India (4th time chairing — 2012, 2016, 2021, 2026)

• New members (2024): Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Indonesia
• BRICS Bank: New Development Bank (NDB), HQ: Shanghai
• Declaration: New Delhi Declaration 2026
• Notable: Modi–Xi bilateral meeting after 7-year gap
