How to Get a Medical Certificate for College Attendance

6 min read · Guides · Back to all guides

Advertisement

If your attendance has dropped below 75% due to illness, most colleges allow you to apply for condonation with a valid medical certificate. Here is exactly what you need and how to get it accepted.

When does a medical certificate actually help?

A medical certificate helps when your attendance shortage was caused by a genuine illness — hospitalization, a prolonged illness that kept you home, surgery, or a serious condition. It typically lets you apply for condonation, which means the college may overlook the shortage or reduce the minimum threshold for that semester.

It does not help retroactively if you simply skipped classes and are now below the cutoff with no illness involved. Colleges and universities are familiar with this and verify accordingly.

What kind of certificate is accepted

Most JNTU-affiliated colleges require a certificate from a Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP) — this means a licensed doctor, not a pharmacist or a medical shop. The certificate should be on the doctor's letterhead and include:

  • Doctor's name, qualification, and registration number
  • Your name and age
  • The illness or condition (general terms are fine — "viral fever", "typhoid", "fracture")
  • The dates you were advised rest — this must cover the dates you were absent
  • Doctor's signature and stamp

A certificate without a registration number or on plain paper is usually rejected. Government hospital certificates are accepted without question. Private clinic certificates from a known local doctor are also generally accepted.

Where to get it

  • Government hospital (best option) — free, carries the most weight with college authorities, and is unquestionable. Ask for a discharge summary if you were admitted, or an outpatient certificate if you visited as an outpatient.
  • Private clinic or nursing home — accepted at most colleges, especially if the doctor is a specialist. Make sure the letterhead has all required details.
  • College health center — if your college has one, their certificate is automatically accepted. Check whether your campus has one before going elsewhere.
Get the certificate during or immediately after the illness — backdated certificates issued weeks later are often questioned.

How to submit it to your college

  1. Write a covering letter — address it to the HOD or Principal, stating your name, roll number, the period of absence, and that you are enclosing a medical certificate.
  2. Attach a photocopy of the original certificate — keep the original with you. Submit the photocopy with the letter.
  3. Submit to your class teacher or HOD — do not just leave it at the office. Hand it directly to the person responsible for attendance records and ask for acknowledgment.
  4. Follow up — after a week, check whether it has been processed. Attendance records are updated periodically and your correction may not reflect immediately.

What if condonation is still not granted

If the shortage is large enough that condonation doesn't bring you to the eligible range, you may be required to appear for a makeup exam in a later cycle, or in some cases, repeat the semester. Check your university's specific rules — JNTU's regulations on this are in the academic regulations document for your batch year.

The best outcome is always to catch the problem early — which means checking your attendance per subject regularly, not waiting for the official notice.
