About

Built because a spreadsheet wasn't going to cut it.

Scholar started the way most useful student tools do: as a personal fix for a personal problem. Attendance in engineering college isn't tracked kindly — it's tracked strictly, subject by subject, with a hard cutoff that decides whether you're even allowed to sit an exam. Missing that number by a percentage point because you didn't know where you stood is one of the most avoidable, and most common, ways a semester goes wrong.

The first version was rough — a single page that just added up presents and absents. What kept it alive past that first week was realizing the same problem repeats everywhere in college life: the timetable that changes every semester and needs re-entering, the assignment deadline that gets buried in a chat thread, the study session that never happens because there's no visible record that it should. Scholar grew one piece at a time to answer each of those, in the order they actually came up.

What we believe about student tools

Most productivity apps are built for offices. Students have a different rhythm — fixed class periods, hard attendance cutoffs, exam windows that don't move, and very little patience for a tool that gets in the way of actually studying. A few things guided every decision in Scholar:

Your data should stay yours

Scholar stores everything — attendance records, tasks, diary entries, your timetable — directly on your own device. There's no account to create and no central server holding a copy of your class schedule. If you use the optional AI features, they run on an API key you provide yourself, so even that stays under your control.

The number should be visible before it matters

Attendance percentage is only useful if you see it in time to act on it. Scholar updates it the moment you mark a period, not at the end of the month.

Setup should happen once

A timetable doesn't change week to week. Neither should the effort of entering it. Fixed period timings and a simple weekly grid mean you set your subjects up once per semester and get on with things.

Who it's for

Scholar was built with engineering and college students in India in mind — the kind of schedule with seven or eight fixed periods a day, strict attendance policies, and exam cycles that come around fast. If that's your week, this was built for you. If your schedule looks different, most of it still holds up: a class timetable, a task list with real deadlines, and an honest attendance number are useful anywhere.

Where it's headed

Scholar is actively developed, mostly in response to the same kind of real, specific problem that started it — a feature request from someone mid-semester who needed something the app didn't do yet. That's still the best way it grows. If there's a piece of your college routine it doesn't handle well, that's exactly the kind of thing worth telling us about.
